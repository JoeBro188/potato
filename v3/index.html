Twelve facts, from space shuttles to sweet-potato impostors.

Nutrition. With the skin on, a plain potato has more potassium than a banana, a solid hit of vitamin C and B6, and almost no fat. A medium baked potato (173 g) has about 161 calories. Values are approximate and based on USDA FoodData Central; fries reflect a typical fast-food preparation. Cooking then chilling potatoes turns some starch into resistant starch, which acts like fiber. Roughly half of the fiber sits in and just under the skin.

Botany. A potato is a stem tuber: its “eyes” are nodes, each holding buds that can sprout an entirely new plant. Kingdom Plantae · Order Solanales · Family Solanaceae (nightshades) · Genus Solanum · Species S. tuberosum. Close relatives: tomato, eggplant, pepper. Origin: the Andes, southern Peru and northwestern Bolivia.

Green means stop. Light-exposed potatoes make chlorophyll, which often signals rising solanine, a bitter glycoalkaloid. Cut away green patches and sprouts. The plant’s leaves, stems and little tomato-like berries are toxic.

History.

World. Potatoes produce more food per hectare than most grains. 1.3B people rely on them as a staple food, and ~85% of the plant is edible human food, compared with roughly half for cereals. Dishes: .

Storage. Stored cool, dark and ventilated, potatoes keep for weeks to months. Very cold storage turns starch into sugar, which can mean sweeter taste and darker browning when fried. Brush off dirt right before cooking.
