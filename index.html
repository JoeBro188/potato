01 — Botany

Not a root.
A swollen stem.

A potato is a stem tuber: an underground stem packed with starch to feed the plant through winter. Its “eyes” are nodes, each holding buds that can sprout an entirely new plant.

KingdomPlantae
OrderSolanales
FamilySolanaceae (nightshades)
GenusSolanum
SpeciesS. tuberosum
Close relativesTomato, eggplant, pepper
OriginAndes, S. Peru & NW Bolivia
⚠️
Green means stop. Light-exposed potatoes make chlorophyll, which often signals rising solanine, a bitter glycoalkaloid. Cut away green patches and sprouts. The plant's leaves, stems and little tomato-like berries are toxic.

03 — Varieties

Pick your potato.

Starch content decides almost everything. Floury potatoes fall apart into fluff, while waxy ones hold their shape. Here are some all-stars.

05 — Cooking

Six ways to glory.

The potato is the most forgiving ingredient in the kitchen. Here are the basics, with the numbers that matter.

2×

The secret to great fries: fry twice

The first fry at ~325°F (160°C) cooks the inside. Rest the fries, then fry again at ~375°F (190°C) to set a glassy, crisp shell. Soaking the cut potatoes first rinses off surface starch so they don't stick together. Very dark browning means more acrylamide, so aim for golden, not brown.

08 — Storage

Keep them cool, dark, calm.

🌑

Dark

Light triggers greening and solanine. Use a paper bag, a cupboard or a basket with a cloth over it.

🌬️

Ventilated

Let them breathe. Plastic bags trap moisture and speed up rot.

🧅

Away from onions

Onions give off gases and moisture that make potatoes sprout faster.

🚿

Don't pre-wash

Moisture invites mold. Brush off the dirt right before cooking.

The storage sweet spot
